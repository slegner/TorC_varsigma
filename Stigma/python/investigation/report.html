The ς=1 Sign Cancellation

Two factors in the modified Friedmann constraint change sign together at ς=1. They divide out, so the radiation coefficients stay real and positive on both sides — and nothing in the algebra records which side it came from.

Question why ς<1 reproduces ς>1-type evolution Method full re-derivation from the Lagrangian Revised 25 Aug 2026 · adds §6–§7

Verdict

No algebra is wrong. The Lagrangian, the Φ elimination, the constraint and the early-time series were rebuilt from scratch and agree with the notebook exactly.

At the radiation fixed point ϖ₀=±1 the two sign-sensitive factors in the constraint are ς−ϖ₀² = ς−1 and (4ς²−1)ϖ₀²+ς(5−8ς) = −(4ς−1)(ς−1). For every ς>¼ these have opposite signs, so their ratio — which is what sets a₁² — is positive on both sides of ς=1 and carries no record of the flip.

Since v₁<0 always, the analytic branch drifts down from 1 in both regimes. For ς>1 that moves it away from ϖ=√ς; for ς<1 it moves straight into it, and that surface is exactly where the Φ elimination divides by zero.

There is a second, upward branch for ς<1 — but it does not rescue the model. The two singular surfaces ϖ=√ς and ϖ=2√ς cross the line ϖ=1 at ς=1 and ς=¼, so for ¼ < ς < 1 the starting point sits strictly between them. Both exits are closed. For ς>1 it lies below the corridor entirely.

§2 From the action to the pole

Where every factor in the reduced equations comes from.

The starting point, on FLRW with ds² = N²dt² − a²dx² and X_ψ = ψ′²/2N²:

These are what generate every special surface in the problem. Term by term:

So there are two distinct surfaces, from two distinct places. ϖ²=4ς is where the effective Planck mass vanishes. ϖ²=ς is where the Φ mass term vanishes — it arises from the competition between ¾Mp²υ₂Φ² and −3σ₂Φ²ψ², and it is the one that matters here.

Φ carries no kinetic term: the coefficient of Φ′ in its own Euler–Lagrange equation vanishes identically, not after simplification. So Φ is auxiliary and is eliminated algebraically:

5 · Eliminating Φ — this is where the pole is createdΦ equation: Φ (ς − ϖ²) a = −2 sgn ς [ (ϖ²−1)a′ + a ϖ ϖ′ ] ⇒ Φ = − 2 sgn ς [ (ϖ²−1)a′ + a ϖ ϖ′ ] ─────────────────────────────── a (ς − ϖ²)

The original action contains no (ς−ϖ²)⁻¹. It appears only here. Back-substituting produces terms in [(ϖ²−1)a′+aϖϖ′]²/(ς−ϖ²), and varying those gives the (ς−ϖ²)⁻² that sits in the denominators of the reduced accelerations.

This also disposes of the square-root sign, structurally rather than by inspection. Φ ∝ sgn, and the Lagrangian is quadratic in Φ plus sgn×(linear in Φ) — so eliminating Φ can only ever produce sgn². Carried symbolically to the end, all three final equations contain sgn only at exponent 2, and Eq(sgn=+1) − Eq(sgn=−1) = 0 exactly.

What remains is the modified Friedmann constraint. In conformal time, with ρ̃ = Ω_r0 + Ω_m0 a + Ω_L0 a⁴:

§3 The cancellation

Why a₁ comes out real and positive on both sides of ς=1.

Evaluate that constraint at the radiation fixed point, ϖ=1, ϖ′=0. Only two structures survive, one on each side:

7 · Both sides carry the same factorleft : (ϖ² − ς)|₍ϖ₌₁₎ = 1 − ς right : [(4ς²−1)ϖ² + ς(5−8ς)]|₍ϖ₌₁₎ = −4ς² + 5ς − 1 = (4ς−1)(1 − ς) ⇒ 4H₀² ς (1−ς) ρ̃ = mυ₂ (4ς−1)(1−ς) a′²

The factor (1−ς) divides out. What is left has no memory of it:

8 · The result, with the sign information gonea′² = 4H₀² ς ρ̃ / [ mυ₂ (4ς−1) ] ⇒ a₁ = 2H₀ √(ς Ω_r0) / √( mυ₂ (4ς−1) ) — positive for every ς > ¼

This is the whole mechanism. The two factors have opposite signs for all ς>¼, so their ratio is positive regardless of which side of ς=1 you are on:

Written with the square root left unsimplified, the same statement reads: for ς<1 the numerator supplies the imaginary unit, for ς>1 the denominator does, and in both cases a′ comes out real.

9 · The same thing, before simplificationa′ = ± 2i H₀ √Ω_r0 √ς · √(ς − ϖ₀²) ───────────────────────────────────────────── √( mυ₂ ϖ₀² [ 5ς − ϖ₀² + 4ς²(−2 + ϖ₀²) ] )
This is why checking that the simplified a₁ is real gives no warning whatsoever. Reality is preserved on both sides, by a different pairing of imaginary factors each time. The simplified expression is a single analytic function of ς on ς>¼; the information distinguishing the two sectors was cancelled, not carried.

A precision about "continuation." What continues analytically across ς=1 is the algebraically simplified radiation solution, as a function of the parameter ς. That is a continuation in parameter space. It is not a trajectory crossing the surface ϖ²=ς in phase space — no solution does that here. The two notions are distinct and only the first is happening at this stage.

§4 ϖ₀ = ±1 is forced

Not a normalisation — the unique regular radiation fixed point.

Impose pure radiation, constant ϖ, and the constraint, with a ∝ τ. The ϖ equation then factors completely:

10 · Pure-radiation ϖ equation with the constraint imposed−48 H₀² Mp² Ω_r0 (ς−1) ς³ · (ϖ₀−1)(ϖ₀+1) · (ς−ϖ₀²) ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── = 0 ϖ₀ ( 5ς − 8ς² − ϖ₀² + 4ς²ϖ₀² )

The roots are ϖ₀ = ±1 and ϖ₀² = ς — and the third is the singular surface, not an interior solution. Independently, the constant-ϖ correspondence polynomial evaluated at w=1/3 factors to 8Mp²mυ₂(ς−1)ς²√x(√x−1)(√x+1)a³, giving x=1 uniquely.

So ϖ₀ cannot be retuned to sit inside the region ϖ²<ς while remaining a radiation fixed point. This closes the last escape route: the ς<1 behaviour cannot be repaired by choosing different initial data.

§5 The early-time coefficients

Unique, linear, and negative — with no ± branch anywhere.

Expand a = Σ aᵢτⁱ (i≥1) and ϖ = 1 + Σ vᵢτⁱ. The two leading orders read:

11 · Leading ordersconstraint, τ⁰ : (ς−1) · [ 4H₀²Ω_r0 ς + mυ₂ a₁²(1−4ς) ] = 0 ϖ equation, τ⁰ : mυ₂ (ς−1)² · a₁ · ( a₂ + a₁v₁ ) = 0 constraint, τ¹ : mυ₂ [ a₁v₁ − (4ς−1)a₂ ] = −H₀² Ω_m0 ς

Both of the last two are linear in {a₂, v₁}, so within this ansatz the solution is unique:

12 · The coefficientsa₁ = 2H₀ √(Ω_r0 ς) / √( mυ₂(4ς−1) ) (a₁>0 = expanding) a₂ = H₀² Ω_m0 / (4 mυ₂) v₁ = −H₀ Ω_m0 √(4ς−1) / ( 8 √(mυ₂ Ω_r0 ς) ) < 0 for all ς > ¼
The scope of this uniqueness. It covers only solutions analytic at τ=0. The ansatz ϖ = 1 + Σvᵢτⁱ excludes logarithmic terms by construction, and §7 shows the system has a second branch that is exactly of that excluded type. "v₁ is unique" is a statement about the Taylor class, not about the solution space.

Note the structure of the leading orders: the constraint's τ⁰ coefficient carries an overall (ς−1), and the ϖ equation's carries (ς−1)². Both degenerate entirely at ς=1, which is the source of the non-uniformity there and of the 1/(ς−1) growth in the higher coefficients.

The notebook holds a second, later expression for this same coefficient carrying an explicit (ς−1) factor, which would flip sign at ς=1. It is not correct. It descends from solving the constraint for a′ — one root of a quadratic, simplified under a fixed sign assumption — followed by a hand-patched term insertion. The value above is the one that follows from the two linear equations.

§6 The corridor

Two singular surfaces, and where ϖ₀=1 sits between them.

The reduced system has two surfaces where it degenerates, from the two places identified in §2:

15 · The two wallsϖ = √ς Φ mass term vanishes — the elimination is singular ϖ = 2√ς effective Planck mass vanishes — the graviton kinetic term flips

Both cross the radiation line ϖ=1, and at telling places: √ς = 1 at ς=1, and 2√ς = 1 at ς=¼. So the starting point ϖ₀=1 lies strictly between the two walls exactly when ¼ < ς < 1 — the whole region of interest. For ς>1 it lies below both.

That is the real asymmetry between the two regimes. It is not that ς<1 drifts the wrong way; it is that ς<1 starts inside a corridor with both ends closed, while ς>1 starts outside it.

§7 The upward branch

It exists — and it is not a cosmology.

The constraint is quadratic in ϖ′, so at ϖ=1 there are two admissible values, not one. In pure radiation the constant term vanishes identically on the a₁ relation, and the quadratic factors:

So the upward branch is real, and the instinct that it should exist was right. The constraint does carry a second root, its sign does flip at ς=1, and it does move away from ϖ²=ς. What it does not do is provide a ς<1 cosmology: it has no early-time limit, and it leaves through the opposite wall almost immediately.

With x_reg = 1 − 3(ς−1)ε/(8ς²) + … and ε = w − 1/3 < 0, for ς<1 the correspondence gives x_reg < 1. So the correspondence and the dynamical solution both move downward from the radiation point. The earlier expectation that the ς<1 branch should move to larger ϖ was a misreading of this sign; v₁<0 is the correct direction.

The discrepancy is a rate, not a direction:

The equilibrium curve never reaches the surface — 0.888 at ς=0.6 stays well above 0.6. But the dynamical solution moves ~23× faster and overshoots it entirely, which is how it reaches ϖ²=ς deep in radiation rather than gently tracking the curve. The correspondence is not an attractor of the dynamical system in either regime, which resolves the tension previously flagged as unexplained.

§9 What this establishes — and what it does not

The boundary of the claim.

Established here. ϖ₀=±1 is the unique regular radiation fixed point. v₁ is unique and negative for all ς>¼. The two factors ς−ϖ₀² and (4ς²−1)ϖ₀²+ς(5−8ς) have opposite signs on either side of ς=1 and cancel in a₁². For ς<1 the downward drift therefore runs into ϖ²=ς, where the Φ elimination is singular; for ς>1 the identical drift moves away from it. All of this is independent of what one calls those inequalities.

Not established here. That either inequality is a no-ghost or stability condition. Both were read off coefficients of the minisuperspace Lagrangian, and that is not sufficient grounds for the terminology:

  • The gravitational scale-factor kinetic term has an unusual sign structure by nature — the conformal mode — so a negative coefficient there is not by itself evidence of a ghost.
  • Φ is auxiliary. The sign of its Φ² coefficient is not automatically a propagating-ghost criterion.

What is verified is an identity: these two inequalities are exactly the sign conditions on those two coefficients, and they coincide with the two constraints already enforced in the numerical pipeline. If those constraints were derived from a full perturbative or Hamiltonian stability analysis elsewhere, then ϖ₀²=1 ⇒ ς>1 is a genuine physical restriction and the conclusion is strong. If they were only ever read off the minisuperspace Lagrangian, that provenance should be settled before the terminology goes into a paper. The dynamical result stands either way — only its interpretation as a stability statement depends on it.

§11 Consequences

What to do with this.

  • Settle the provenance of the two inequalities first. Everything downstream — whether ς>1 is a physical requirement or merely the region where this particular solution survives — turns on that one question.
  • Both walls matter, not just ϖ²=ς. The corridor ¼<ς<1 is bounded below by the elimination singularity and above by the effective-Planck-mass surface. Only the lower wall stops the integrator; the upper one is crossed silently, so it needs its own monitor.
  • Treat the algebraically real x_R root with suspicion. It is a genuine real root of the ϖ field equation, which is why it looked like a live ς<1 branch, but it sits at ϖ²>ς, on the far side of the surface from the region where the elimination is valid.
  • Carry the individual signs, not just the simplified expression. The failure mode is silent precisely because simplification cancels the two flips. Monitoring sign(ς−ϖ²) and the bracket separately would have caught this at once — and will catch it again if ϖ approaches √ς from the other side.
  • Re-examine the ς>1 drift on its own terms. ϖ² falling from 1 to 0.45 by τ=0.155 is ~15× faster than the quasi-static equilibrium and is not obviously physical. The surface was masking this question, not answering it.
  • Discard the later ϖ₁ chain and everything downstream of the hand-patched coefficient list.

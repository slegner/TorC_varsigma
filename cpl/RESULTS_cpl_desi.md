# CPL (w0waCDM) vs DESI DR2 BAO — nested sampling results

Produced by `cpl/cpl_nss.py` (BlackJAX nested slice sampling, 500 live points,
`num_inner_steps = 5*ndim`, seed 0), against the 13-point DESI DR2 BAO vector in
`../../desidr2/`.

Sampler settings and the priors on the shared parameters (`h0rd`, `omega_m`)
match `Stigma/python/stigma_nss.py`, so the log-evidences below are directly
comparable to a Stigma Q-torsion run.

## Posteriors (real DESI DR2 data)

| | ΛCDM | CPL (w0waCDM) |
|---|---|---|
| log Z | −7.15 ± 0.13 | −8.52 ± 0.15 |
| D_KL | 7.69 | 10.49 |
| H₀r_d [km/s] | 10155 (+68 / −75) | 9408 (+413 / −279) |
| Ω_m | 0.2970 (+0.0092 / −0.0081) | 0.359 (+0.026 / −0.041) |
| w₀ | −1 (fixed) | −0.434 (+0.231 / −0.336) |
| w_a | 0 (fixed) | −1.85 (+1.23 / −0.78) |
| χ²_min (13 pts) | 10.53 | 5.77 |

**log B(CPL / ΛCDM) = −1.35 ± 0.20.**

Interpretation: CPL improves the fit by Δχ² ≈ 4.8 for two extra parameters, but
the Occam penalty from the wide `w0`, `wa` priors outweighs it, so DESI DR2 BAO
*alone* mildly favours ΛCDM on Bayesian evidence. This is the expected result —
the well-known w0waCDM preference in the DESI papers appears only once SNe
and/or CMB are added.

## Cross-checks

- ΛCDM posterior reproduces the published DESI DR2 values
  (Ω_m = 0.2975 ± 0.0086, H₀r_d ≈ 10154 km/s).
- CPL posterior reproduces the published DESI DR2 BAO-only w0waCDM values
  (Ω_m ≈ 0.353, w₀ ≈ −0.42, w_a ≈ −1.75).
- Noiseless mock recovery (`--mock --mock-noiseless`, fiducial
  H₀r_d = 10145, Ω_m = 0.30, w₀ = −0.90, w_a = −0.50) returns
  10157, 0.2987, −0.910, −0.491 with χ²_min = 0.03 — the pipeline is unbiased.

## Caveats

- `w_a` runs into its prior edge at −3; the BAO-only `w_a` constraint is
  prior-limited, so log B depends on the assumed `w0`/`wa` prior ranges. Those
  ranges were taken from `../../common.py` (`w0 ~ U[-3,1]`,
  `wa ~ U[-3,2]`, with `w0 + wa < 0`) to stay consistent with the polychord
  runs in that directory.
- Ω_r is fixed at 8.24e-5, matching `equations_jax.OMEGA_R`.
- `r_d` is marginalised through the single combination `h0rd = H0 * r_d`; no
  CAMB sound-horizon calculation enters, exactly as in `stigma_nss.py`.

# Three-way comparison: CPL vs TorC (1/ς² prior) vs ΛCDM

Produced by `cpl/cpl_torc_compare.py`, which overlays the CPL chains on the
TorC power-law-prior chain
`../chains/torc_ns_julia_1000_powerlaw.npz`.

## Matched prior box

log Z only means something relative to a prior. The TorC runs use
Ω_m ~ U[0.01, 0.99] and H₀r_d ~ U[3650, 18250]
(`../torc/priors.py`), which is wider in Ω_m than the box used in
the first half of this document. `cpl_nss.py --prior-box torc` reproduces it,
and every number below is in that box.

| model | log Z | Δ log Z vs ΛCDM |
|---|---|---|
| ΛCDM | −7.58 ± 0.09 | 0 |
| TorC, 1/ς² prior | −8.03 ± 0.10 | −0.45 |
| CPL (w₀w_aCDM) | −9.06 ± 0.11 | −1.48 |

**Cross-pipeline validation:** ΛCDM run through `cpl_nss.py` in this box gives
−7.58 ± 0.09 against TorC's own independent ΛCDM value of −7.734 ± 0.094 —
agreement within 1σ, from two separate codes (JAX/analytic here, JAX/diffrax
ODE there) on the same data.

## Reading

- ΛCDM is preferred over both extensions, but only TorC's margin is small
  enough to be a genuine dead heat (|Δ log Z| = 0.45, inconclusive on the
  Jeffreys scale). CPL's −1.48 is a "weak" preference for ΛCDM and no more.
- CPL pays a larger Occam penalty than TorC for the same data: two wide extra
  parameters (w₀, w_a) versus one 1/ς²-weighted parameter that concentrates
  its prior mass near the ΛCDM limit.
- Neither model is detected. BAO alone does not distinguish them.

## Evidence decomposition

Nested sampling splits the evidence exactly:

**log Z = <log L>_P - D_KL**

— how well the model can fit (posterior-averaged log-likelihood) minus what it
paid to get there (information gained from prior to posterior, i.e. the Occam
factor, in nats). A model can lose either by fitting worse or by needing more
prior compression; the split says which. `d_G` is the Bayesian model
dimensionality — the number of parameters the data actually constrains.

| model | log Z | <log L>_P | D_KL | d_G | N sampled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ΛCDM | −7.57 ± 0.10 | 0.60 ± 0.02 | 8.17 ± 0.09 | 1.90 ± 0.06 | 2 |
| TorC (1/ς²) | −8.03 ± 0.09 | 0.71 ± 0.02 | 8.74 ± 0.09 | 2.21 ± 0.06 | 4 |
| CPL (w₀w_aCDM) | −9.04 ± 0.11 | 2.02 ± 0.03 | 11.06 ± 0.11 | 3.82 ± 0.09 | 4 |

Differences vs ΛCDM (positive favours the extension):

| model | Δ log Z | Δ<log L>_P | Δ D_KL |
|---|---|---|---|
| TorC (1/ς²) | −0.45 ± 0.13 | **+0.12 ± 0.03** | **+0.57 ± 0.13** |
| CPL | −1.46 ± 0.15 | **+1.43 ± 0.04** | **+2.89 ± 0.14** |

### What the split says

- **CPL genuinely fits better.** It gains +1.43 nats of posterior-averaged
  likelihood over ΛCDM — a real improvement, consistent with the Δχ² ≈ 4.8 seen
  earlier. It loses on evidence purely because it pays +2.89 nats of Occam
  factor to buy it. The verdict against CPL is a complexity penalty, not a bad fit.
- **TorC barely moves the fit at all** (+0.12 nats) but also barely pays
  (+0.57 nats). It sits close to ΛCDM in both terms, which is why its Δ log Z is
  a dead heat rather than a defeat.
- **d_G is the reason for the asymmetry.** Both extensions sample 4 parameters,
  but the data constrain only ~2.2 directions in TorC versus ~3.8 in CPL. TorC's
  Ω_m and OmL are largely unconstrained (visible as the broad pink Ω_m marginal
  in the corner plot), and unconstrained directions cost almost nothing in
  D_KL — the 1/ς² prior also concentrates mass near the ΛCDM limit ς → 1.
  CPL's w₀ and w_a are both constrained and both wide-prior, so they cost.

Consistency checks: the decomposition closes to machine precision
(log Z + D_KL − <log L>_P = 0 for all three), and the TorC log Z recomputed
here from its dead points reproduces the stored value (−8.026) exactly.
